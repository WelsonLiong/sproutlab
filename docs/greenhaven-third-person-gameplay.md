# Greenhaven third person gameplay

This document describes the earlier ten-discovery sequence, which is still accessible as legacy content. The revised campaign plan and new Lost Stream slice are described in `nico-dying-forest-revised-plan.md`.

Greenhaven now follows Nico from behind. WASD and arrow keys move relative to the camera; dragging open scenery turns the view. On touch screens, the directional pad moves Nico and a drag on the world turns the view. E uses nearby equipment. The camera follows Nico through all three clearings. The mobile mirror view shows both mirrors and the target leaf above the mission panel.

The creek practice is performed in the world: pick up the loose handle, fit it to the gate, turn the wooden channel by dragging or pressing E beside it, then return to the gate and release the water. Incorrect routes still visibly send water to the pond or stump and can be retried. The old route buttons are no longer shown during play.

Assessed tasks now use the 3D objects as their primary controls. The pupil drags mirrors to aim the beam, carries water and starch samples to their destinations, drags a gas molecule to the leaf opening, shines the lamp on both leaf areas, operates the water-plant collector and splint, drags food-chain figures into order, and drags a patch to the rabbit shelter. Keyboard interaction remains available: walk to an object, press E, then use E at its destination where applicable. The two reasoning conversations and the gas identification use readable choices. Mirror, leaf comparison and food-chain tasks retain a deliberate check after physical setup; moving parts alone does not consume a mark.

First committed tests still award one mark when correct. Wrong configurations give explanatory feedback and remain retryable, while saved attempts, retakes and certificates retain their existing scoring rules. The science remains within Year 4 DSKP 4.2.1–4.2.4; the water-plant apparatus is a simplified observation model.

Verification exercised third person camera orbit, mirror dragging, CO₂ delivery, patch planting, desktop and mobile layout, and browser errors. The creek check exercised handle pickup, fitting, channel aiming and releasing water to roots. The existing marks check passed. Browser checks used a local HTTP preview and test-only learner storage; classroom usability remains to be checked with pupils.
